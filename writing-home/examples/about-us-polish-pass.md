# About Us, polish pass

This is the rewrite of `src/content/about/-index.md` after the first transcript-style draft was rejected. It demonstrates the same fidelity to the founder voice and source transcript, in professional copy register.

Use this example when the drift to capture is "transcript-style cadence applied to every section." The lesson is that voice fidelity and copy polish are different disciplines.

## The draft (current production copy)

### Title
The foundation Maryland businesses and the agencies serving them run on. Built since 2015.

### Since 2015
Since 2015, we have been working with local Maryland businesses. What we found was not a business problem or a customer problem. It was a tools problem.

Mid-market and small businesses were building on platforms designed for someone else, at a different scale. WordPress. Go High Level. The usual stack. They were built to be quick to set up, not built to scale. Every "quick change" became a support ticket. The same platform that got a business started became the thing holding it back, because it was never built to bend.

The enterprises had access to blazing-fast tools, real data pipelines, and open-source engineering. The local layer never inherited any of it. Maryland Insights is the platform that closes that gap, engineered around how Maryland businesses and the agencies serving them actually operate, so reaching the community and scaling does not mean fighting your own technology to do it.

### Who We Are
We're not a marketing agency. We're not a SaaS platform. We're not a bespoke software agency. We're the foundation layer.

Maryland Insights is built by a team of data science and go-to-market engineers. The platform is what they build: hosting, maintenance, integrations, and the marketing infrastructure underneath it, on the same open-source stack the enterprises have been running for years.

For Maryland businesses, that means one connected system engineered to work together. Not a stack of separate logins bolted together after the fact. The website, the local visibility, the lead capture, the follow-up, on one platform. When a business grows, adds a location, or changes how it sells, the platform adapts with it.

For Maryland agencies, that means direct access to the platform your clients run on. The ability to configure it, extend it, and deliver the exact marketing experience each client is paying for, instead of reselling a rigid product and hoping it fits. When something new is needed, the engineers building the platform are in the room, not a ticket queue.

We are not here to replace what agencies or businesses already have. We are here to make what they have finally work the way it was supposed to. Augmentation, not replacement.

### Mission
Every Maryland business should be able to grow at scale, from Baltimore City to the Eastern Shore.

When the platform handles the infrastructure, Maryland businesses and the agencies serving them get their time back for the work that actually differentiates them. The strategy. The creativity. The relationship with the customer. The parts that cannot be automated or engineered away.

### Vision
A Maryland where every local business runs like a well-oiled machine and has the infrastructure to multiply.

Where the data Maryland residents are already generating finds its way to the businesses serving them first, before a national brand with a bigger ad budget gets there. Where the right foundation lets a Maryland business be found by the people already looking for it.

### Facts
- Foundation. Hosting, maintenance, and integrations on the open-source engineering stack the enterprises have been running for years. The platform your business operates on.
- Decision Fuel. Real-time data on who is visiting, what they are doing, and what is converting. Insight, not dashboards.
- Maryland Visibility. Local visibility that earns its position. Granular Maryland data the national brands cannot fake, generated by your customers and your community.
- Time Back. Time back for strategy, creative, and the customer relationships that actually differentiate your business.

### Story cards
- What We Do. We provide the platform. Hosting, maintenance, and integrations on the latest open-source stack. Real infrastructure behind every site. Uptime you can count on. Security patched before it becomes a problem. Integrations that connect to whatever tools you or your clients already run on. No closed system locking you in to a single vendor's roadmap.
- What We Do Not Do. We are not a marketing agency. We do not run campaigns. We do not promise direct growth results, because the platform enables them. We do not build or design websites. We do not run or manage ads.
- How. It starts with a conversation, not a sales pitch. We talk through what is actually involved. Migrating off what you are running today. What your goals are. What data needs to carry over. How you want reporting to work. What integrations you or your clients already depend on. From there, the agreement is in writing. What we are building. What it costs. What you can expect. No surprises once we start. Once it is settled, we move into partnership. The platform gets built around your answers, not a template you have to adapt to.

## What changed from the transcript-style draft

| In the first draft | In the polish pass | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment paragraphs throughout | Full declarative sentences with proper breaks | Fragment cadence works as a kicker, not as the structural rhythm |
| "So your business, or your agency's clients, never get lost or lose presence in the digital marketing space" | Split into "For Maryland businesses..." and "For Maryland agencies..." sections | Hedging for both audiences dilutes for both |
| Build / Optimize / Rank / Grow facts | Foundation / Decision Fuel / Maryland Visibility / Time Back | The founder's mental model, not a borrowed framework |
| "What We Do Not Do" ran 130 words | "What We Do Not Do" is now four sentences, boundary first | The boundary is the headline of the card |
| The first draft read like transcribed speech | The polish pass reads like the founder wrote it on a good day | Same signature phrases, professional register |

## What this example demonstrates

- Voice fidelity and copy polish are different disciplines. Capture both.
- Verbatim signature phrases preserve voice. Professional copy register is the surface.
- Audience-separated framing replaces hedging-for-both.
- Founder's actual mental model (foundation, decision fuel, Maryland visibility, time back) replaces borrowed pillar frameworks.
- Boundary-drawing belongs at the top of the boundary card, not buried.
- The signature cadence is used sparingly. Three-beat negative as opener, run-on feature list once in "Since 2015," three-beat fragments as closing beats in Mission.

## What not to imitate mechanically

- The specific labels on the facts cards. Foundation, Decision Fuel, Maryland Visibility, Time Back are the founder's mental model as of this draft. They may shift as the platform evolves.
- The exact word count of each paragraph. The discipline is that each section earns its place. The specific lengths will vary by piece.
